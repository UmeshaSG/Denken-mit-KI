Framework / Working draft

Thinking with AI

Seed. Ground. Spar. Steer. Stress. Settle.

By Velora · November 2026

I asked AI to help me build a framework for AI-native thinking.

It gave me one. Then another. Then a third.

All of them were good. None of them answered my question.

I wanted to know how to think with AI. AI kept telling me how to hand work to AI. Close topic. Different question.

It took me several rounds of pushing back before it understood what I meant.

That moment is what this framework is about.

Asking AI is not thinking with AI

Most of us use AI to get answers. Ask, receive, move on.

Thinking with AI is different. You bring the idea. AI stretches it, pushes on it, and shows you what you missed. Then you decide what survives.

It sounds simple. In practice, three things go wrong.

  • AI drifts. It answers the question it understood, not the one you meant. If you don't notice, you follow it somewhere else.
  • AI agrees. Push back and it often folds. That feels like winning. It isn't.
  • AI fills the gaps. If you don't say what it should think from, it makes up its own ground.

So I needed a method. Something with an order, and a check at every step.

AI widens. You narrow.

The framework

01Seed

Start with your own thought. Or your own problem. Half-formed is fine.

If you ask AI for starting ideas, that's okay. But you pick the one worth pursuing.

The starting point is your choice, not AI's.

02Ground

Decide what AI thinks from before you start. Otherwise it builds its own context on the fly.

  • Your base. The sources, notes and data you trust.
  • Challengers. Ask AI to find the strongest credible sources against your idea. Every source stated. No exceptions.
  • Approve. You check the challengers. Keep the real ones. Drop the weak or invented ones.
  • Limits. AI flags anything it says from outside the approved ground.

Why let AI find the challengers? Because you won't. We all pick sources that agree with us. AI is good at finding what you'd skip. You are good at judging what's credible. That's the split.

You can list what AI is thinking from, including at least one verified challenger you didn't find yourself.
The think loop · repeat as needed

03Spar

Let AI expand, reframe and propose. Then push back on what doesn't fit.

One warning. If AI agrees after you push back, that is not proof you were right. AI folds easily. Treat agreement as no signal.

You have rejected or reshaped at least part of what AI offered.

04Steer

This is the move most people skip. Every few turns, ask AI one question:

"What question do you think I'm asking?"

If the answer doesn't match yours, restate your intent. Drift is quiet. You only catch it if you check.

You can say in one line what you're exploring, and the conversation matches it.

05Stress

Ask AI to attack the idea before you share your own view. Use the challengers. Check against outside sources, especially in a field you don't know well.

If Stress exposes a gap in your ground, go back. Add the missing sources. Approve them. Keep thinking.

You know the idea's weakest point, and you have an answer for it. That's when you leave the loop.

06Settle

You decide what stays. You write it in your own voice.

Then the real test. Close the chat. Explain the idea to someone. If you can't, you haven't settled it. AI has.

You can defend it without AI in the room.

Signs it's going wrong

  • You accept the first answer.
  • AI drifts, and you follow.
  • AI agrees, and you take it as proof.
  • Your sources only agree with you.
  • You can't explain the idea without AI's words.

What's new here, and what isn't

None of these moves are new on their own.

Thinking before you prompt is common advice. Using AI as a sparring partner is well established. Grounding AI in your own sources is what tools like NotebookLM are built for.

What I haven't seen is the order. And a check at every step. And a named move for drift.

That's the part I'm offering. Use it. Break it. Tell me where it fails.
